The [treasury](/reference/glossary#treasury) holds the funds the tribe owns together. Its money moves only when members pass a proposal. No single person, including the people who started the tribe, can spend it on their own.

For crypto natives: an onchain treasury contract that custodies the tribe's assets and runs the actions of passed proposals. Every payment in and out is public.

## What the treasury holds

- **Funds**, such as XLM or USDC. The Treasury page lists them under "Treasury balances".
- **Membership tokens** the tribe owns, for example a token from an auction nobody bid on. They appear under "Tokens the treasury holds".

## How funds come in

- Each auction sale pays the winning bid to the treasury.
- Fixed-price sales from the market pay the treasury.
- Paid invite lists pay the treasury. In a launch drive, payments wait in escrow until the tribe launches.
- The market's resale fee, if the tribe sets one, goes to the treasury.
- Anyone can send XLM or USDC from their wallet with "Add funds". It then belongs to the tribe and is spent only by vote.

## How funds move

A payment is an action in a proposal. On the Treasury page, "Propose a payout" adds one to your proposal draft. Then the normal lifecycle applies:

1. Members vote, and the proposal passes.
2. Any wallet queues it, and the [safety delay](/reference/glossary#safety-delay) starts.
3. When the delay ends, any wallet executes it. The treasury makes each payment in the order the proposal lists.

"Execution history" on the Treasury page shows what has run.

## Why no single person can move funds

The treasury contract has no function that pays out on request. It acts only by running a proposal that the tribe's Governor marks as passed, queued, and past its safety delay. The person who executes the proposal pays the network fee but chooses nothing: the actions are the ones members voted on.

From launch onwards, the treasury is also the admin of every contract the tribe runs. So changing a voting rule, an auction setting, or a module is also a treasury action, and it also needs a passed proposal. Before launch the treasury cannot run anything.

Tokens the treasury holds carry no votes. The treasury can't vote with them, and they don't count towards quorum. They regain a vote only when a proposal sends them to someone.

### In practice

Amara proposes paying 2,400 XLM to Kenji for design work. The proposal passes. Anyone queues it, and after the tribe's 1-hour safety delay, anyone executes it. The 2,400 XLM moves from the treasury to Kenji's wallet in that transaction. Until then, it stays in the treasury.

## What the treasury doesn't protect against

- **A majority vote.** A proposal that passes can spend anything the treasury holds. The protection is that members can read every action, and that the safety delay gives time to review it.
- **No veto after queueing.** Once a proposal is queued, only expiry stops it.
- **The asset's own issuer.** Some Stellar assets let their issuer freeze or reclaim balances. Funds held in such an asset depend on that issuer. Tribes should set prices and make payments in assets without these powers, such as XLM.
