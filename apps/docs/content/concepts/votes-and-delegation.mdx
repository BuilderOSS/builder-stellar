## In plain words

Each membership token is one vote. On each proposal, you can vote For, Against, or Abstain, and give a reason that other members can read.

Your votes on a proposal are counted from a fixed moment: immediately before the proposal was created. Tokens you receive after that moment count on later proposals, not on that one.

You can also let someone else vote for you. That person is your [delegate](/reference/glossary#delegate). You keep your tokens, and you can choose a new delegate, or vote for yourself again, at any time.

## How a vote is counted

- **For** supports the proposal.
- **Against** opposes it.
- **Abstain** counts towards the [quorum](/reference/glossary#quorum) but supports neither side.

A proposal passes when its For and Abstain votes together reach the quorum and it has more For votes than Against votes. The quorum is a share of all the votes that exist, set by the tribe, such as 10%. See [Proposals](/concepts/proposals).

You can vote once on each proposal. You can't change your vote after it is cast. On the proposal page, the app shows **You have 3 votes on this proposal**, and the vote button names exactly what you will cast, such as **Cast 3 votes For**.

## Why votes are counted at a fixed point

The DAO term for this fixed point is a **snapshot**. When someone creates a proposal, the governor reads everyone's votes as they were at the ledger immediately before it. A ledger is one step in the Stellar network's record, and a new one closes about every 5 seconds. The proposal page shows this as **Voting power snapshot**, with the ledger number.

A snapshot keeps a vote fair:

- **A token can't vote twice.** If you vote and then pass your token to someone else, the new holder has no votes on that proposal.
- **Votes can't be bought for one decision.** Buying tokens after a proposal appears does not change its result.
- **The bar is fixed.** The quorum is calculated once, from the votes that existed at the snapshot, and stored with the proposal.

The wait before voting opens, called the voting delay, is a notice period. It gives members time to read the proposal. It does not move the snapshot.

Tokens held by the tribe's treasury, auction, or market carry no votes, so they are not part of the snapshot or the quorum.

## Delegation

To delegate means to let another address vote with your tokens. In the DAO term, your voting power moves, and your NFTs stay in your wallet.

- **It covers every token you hold in that tribe**, not only one.
- **It starts working at once for new proposals.** Proposals that already exist keep their snapshot.
- **A delegate needs no tokens.** Votes can be delegated to any Stellar address, unless the tribe allows delegation only to members.
- **It does not pass along.** Your delegate's count includes the votes delegated to them, and leaves out their own tokens if they delegated those to someone else.
- **You can take it back.** Select **Vote for myself again** on the delegation form.

When you first receive a token, its votes go to you automatically. Delegation is needed only if you want someone else to vote for you.

If you delegate your votes, you have no votes to cast yourself on proposals created after that. Your delegate votes with them instead.

To delegate, open the **You** sheet, select **View your tokens and delegation**, then select **Delegate votes**. The **Votes go to** line in the **You** sheet shows whether your votes go to you or to a delegate. See [Delegate your votes](/guides/members/delegate).

## Tribes with another vote source

Most tribes count votes from their membership tokens. A tribe can instead be created with another vote source that the platform has approved. In that tribe, votes come from that source, and delegating a token does not move them. See [Vote sources](/concepts/vote-sources).
